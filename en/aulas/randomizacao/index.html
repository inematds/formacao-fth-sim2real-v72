Lesson 2 · 16 min

Domain randomization: training for the varied world

If the simulator never gets the world right, you train the policy in many randomly drawn worlds. In this lesson you read the ranges Unitree itself randomized when training the policy, draw 20 worlds inside them and 20 outside, and measure the difference. The policy handles what it saw in training and fails just past it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Where randomization lives in a real project: the Isaac Lab events
  2. 2The G1 policy's ranges: friction, mass, and push (and what was left out)
  3. 320/20 inside the range, 8/20 outside it
  4. 4Why randomizing everything to the max doesn't work either

Goal: read the randomization ranges of a real training run and measure the policy's survival inside and outside them.

01 · From randomized training to the out-of-range test

Click each block.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Randomization is not "more is better": it's covering the world where the robot will walk.

02 · What Unitree randomized (and what it didn't)

Read in velocity_env_cfg.py, unitree_rl_lab 4960b84 (Nov 19, 2025). Click each item.

1. Friction

physics_material event, at startup: static and dynamic friction between 0.3 and 1.0 on all of the robot's bodies.

In this lesson's out-of-range draw, the worlds with friction near 0.2 (2 and 5) fall; the floor of the training range is the floor the policy has seen.

2. Mass

add_base_mass event: adds −1 to +3 kg to the torso_link. A real payload (extra battery, camera, gripper) can go beyond that.

3. Push

push_robot event, every 5 s: changes the base velocity by up to ±0.5 m/s in x and y. The kit applies the equivalent as a 0.1 s force on the torso.

There is also noise in the observations (angular velocity, gravity, joints).

4. Outside

There is no randomization of kp, kd or latency (delay) in this task. In lesson 1, kp alone already changed the gait (0.75 m/s when asking for 0.50); latency (delay) matters under a push (lesson 4).

Other projects randomize gains and latency (delay); what matters is knowing what your training covered before you power on the robot.

04 · Sample and count

randomizar.py samples 20 worlds per range with a fixed seed (7) and runs 10 s of walking in each one, with the push at t = 3 s.

Terminal · inside and outside the range
F=${FTH:-$HOME/fth}; cd $F/sim2real && . $F/.venv/bin/activate
python3 kit/randomizar.py --seeds 20
Terminal · the first worlds outside the range, one by one
F=${FTH:-$HOME/fth}; cd $F/sim2real && . $F/.venv/bin/activate
python3 kit/randomizar.py --seeds 20 --faixa fora --detalhe | head -6
Example from the repository
faixa treino  sobreviveu: 20/20
faixa fora    sobreviveu: 8/20

Inside the training range, 20 out of 20. Outside, 8 out of 20.

   1 atrito=0.45 massa=7.38 kp=0.91 atraso_ms=6 empurrão 172 N → de pé
   2 atrito=0.20 massa=6.93 kp=0.92 atraso_ms=14 empurrão 119 N → caiu
   3 atrito=0.30 massa=4.67 kp=0.80 atraso_ms=16 empurrão 219 N → caiu
   4 atrito=0.45 massa=7.93 kp=0.69 atraso_ms=4 empurrão 194 N → caiu
   5 atrito=0.21 massa=5.09 kp=0.79 atraso_ms=28 empurrão 50 N → caiu
   6 atrito=0.40 massa=3.49 kp=0.60 atraso_ms=6 empurrão 131 N → caiu

World 1 has a mass of 7.4 kg and stays up; world 4, at 7.9 kg, falls; world 6 has friction 0.40 and falls. No single parameter explains the score: outside the range, it is the combination. Looking at just one world and blaming one parameter is the common mistake; compare scores and, to claim a cause, vary one parameter at a time (E2).

06 · The E2 range

Same ranges as the training, plus kp between 0.5 and 0.8.

cat > $F/so-kp.json <<'J'
{"nome": "so-kp", "atrito": [0.3, 1.0], "massa": [-1.0, 3.0], "kp": [0.5, 0.8], "empurrao_vel": [-0.5, 0.5]}
J
python3 kit/randomizar.py --seeds 20 --faixa $F/so-kp.json
Example from the repository
faixa so-kp   sobreviveu: 11/20

Just adding kp, which the training did not vary, knocks down 9 out of 20.
